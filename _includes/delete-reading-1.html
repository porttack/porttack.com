1. In early February 2026, Nancy Guthrie, who is 84, was reported missing from her home in Arizona. The case is still open. This reading is not about the crime. It is about one camera.

2. Her front door had a Google Nest doorbell camera. The county sheriff said the camera was disconnected at 1:47 a.m. He also said she did not have a paid subscription that would have saved video.

3. More than a week after she disappeared, the FBI released video from that camera. FBI Director Kash Patel wrote on X: "The video was recovered from residual data located in backend systems." Months later he told Fox News that "there was a chance that it was located in their cache just before it was being deleted."

4. Google's own Nest safety page says this about its cameras:

"You can access, review, and delete this footage at any time."
"Some models of our cameras support recording video footage while offline."
"That means you may not see a visual indicator when your camera is sending the video footage to our servers."

5. The same page says a visual indicator would have been visible when the camera was actually recording. It does not say how long video is kept if you do not pay for a subscription.

6. Here is what is still unknown. NBC News reported that it was unclear how the FBI collected the video, and whether Google stored video from a device without a subscription. Google did not answer reporters' questions about how the footage was captured or recovered.

7. In many computer systems, pressing Delete does not erase anything right away. The system marks the data as deleted and removes it later.
