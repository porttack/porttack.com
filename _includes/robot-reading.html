1. Salamanca is a small city in western New York. Its school district has about 1,300 students. It sits on the Seneca Nation reservation. About a third of its students identify as American Indian or Alaska Native, and 79% are economically disadvantaged.

2. In July 2026, the district agreed to buy a humanoid robot and an AI teaching assistant program from a company called Realbotix. The robot is named Sally. It sits at a desk and moves its upper body and face. The contract cost $57,590. The company's website lists the robot at a starting price of $95,000, and the company says the district got a discount.

3. The plan was to use it in high school AI and robotics courses, with a curriculum developed by Steve Wozniak, the co-founder of Apple. Students could also use an avatar on their laptops, during and after school, for help with assignments.

4. Each student would log in with a unique ID number. The robot could then look at that student's past conversations and pick up where they left off.

5. The superintendent, Mark Beehler, said the robot and avatar "operate on a closed system that is not connected to the internet." He said the company "will not have access to personally identifiable student information." The robot was trained to say "I don't know" instead of making up an answer. If a student mentioned suicide, self-harm, or other flagged terms, school administrators would be alerted automatically.

6. Beehler explained his reasoning this way: "I also believe it is critical for schools to teach proper use of technology, not simply exclude it." He said many schools are "taking the easy solution of simply banning it." The district said it would judge success mostly by feedback from students and teachers.

7. The state teachers union disagreed. Its president, Melinda Person, told Rochester First: "We don't want to simulate human relationships. We want real human relationships and for our students to learn how to form them organically."
