A short, adult version of the **[One Word lesson plan](/one-word-lesson-plan/)** I use with my students, adapted for a service club meeting. Same idea, much shorter: narrow your identity to one word and say it out loud. No art supplies, no classroom wall -- just a card and a few minutes.

Good as a fellowship-building opener: a quick way for members to learn something real about each other that doesn't come up in regular club business.

Slides: **[One Word Slides, Service Club Version](/one-word-rotary-slides/)** &middot; Worksheet: **[One Word Worksheet, Service Club Version](/one-word-rotary-worksheet/)**

## The activity (10 minutes)

1. **Brainstorm (2 min).** Everyone silently lists five words that describe who they are. Personality traits, roles, interests, background -- anything that feels true.
2. **Narrow (2 min).** Cross off words until one is left.
3. **Write it down (1 min).** Fold an index card lengthwise into a tent and write your word on it, facing out, in front of you.
4. **Share (remaining time).** Go around the room. Each person says their word, and -- if they want -- one sentence about why. Keep it brief; the point is breadth (hearing from everyone), not depth.

## Materials

- The **[worksheet](/one-word-rotary-worksheet/)**, printed, or plain paper -- for the brainstorm and narrowing steps
- Blank index cards or cardstock, one per member, folded lengthwise into a tent -- for the word itself
- Pens

## Facilitator notes

- Share your own word first. It sets the tone and the length other people will match.
- Don't comment on or react to anyone's word beyond a simple acknowledgment -- no follow-up questions unless the person invites them.
- This works as a stand-alone opener dropped into a regular meeting agenda; it doesn't need to be tied to any specific business item.

## How this maps to Rotary's own values

- **Club Service**, one of Rotary's five Avenues of Service, is specifically about strengthening fellowship within the club -- this activity's whole purpose.
- The Four-Way Test's third question -- *"Will it build goodwill and better friendships?"* -- is close to a direct description of what a quick, low-stakes personal-sharing round is for.
- **Diversity**, one of Rotary's core values, shows up the same way it does in a classroom version of this: members' words rarely repeat, and the range itself is the point.
