## Overview

Students explore personal identity through a structured process of self-reflection, culminating in selecting one word that represents their core identity and contributing to a collaborative classroom word wall.

## Lesson Plan (3 short days, 1 work day)

### Day 1: Identity Brainstorming (15 minutes)

**Learning objectives.** Students will be able to:

- Identify and articulate 5 personal characteristics using appropriate vocabulary
- Demonstrate self-awareness by reflecting on personal identity components
- Participate respectfully in a safe, inclusive classroom environment

**Activities**

- *Opening (3 min).* Hand out the One Word worksheet. Explain: "We're building OUR classroom space together. Today starts with understanding who we are as individuals."
- *Teacher modeling (4 min).* Draw a bubble map on the board with your name in the center. Think aloud while brainstorming: "I could put 'curious' because I love learning new things... 'gamer' because I spend too much time on Steam... 'teacher' because that's my profession..." Share your 5 final words and briefly explain each choice.
- *Instructions and guidelines (3 min).* Word categories: personality traits, hobbies, background/culture, goals, talents, values. Format flexibility: single words work best, but compound identities are welcome (Mexican-American, gamer-artist). Safe space rules: share only what you're comfortable with, use a "code word" for private aspects if needed, never suggest words for others or comment on their choices. Process preview: "Today we brainstorm 5 words. Tomorrow we'll narrow to fewer."
- *Student work time (5 min).* Students complete bubble maps independently. Circulate and offer encouragement, not suggestions. Remind students this isn't a race.
- *Quick check-in (optional).* If time allows: "Turn to someone near you and share just one word you chose."
- Collect the worksheets.

### Day 2: Strategic Elimination (15 minutes)

**Learning objectives.** Students will be able to:

- Apply critical thinking to prioritize personal characteristics
- Communicate reasoning behind decision-making processes
- Reflect on identity choices with increasing self-awareness

**Prep.** Review worksheets. Know which students you might need to talk to.

**Activities**

- *Modeling.* Teacher re-writes words, but different this time (to show that change is okay). Model: cross off one of your 5 words on the board. Think aloud: "I'm crossing off 'gamer' because while I enjoy games, 'curious' better captures how I approach both gaming AND teaching."
- *Review and revisions (3 min).* Return worksheets, allow time for any changes. "You can modify your words anytime -- identity isn't fixed."
- *Process introduction (2 min).* "Today we narrow to 2 words through careful elimination." "This mirrors how we make tough decisions in life and work."
- *Guided elimination, round 1 (4 min).* Students eliminate one word. Volunteers share: "What word did you cross off?" "What made that elimination easy or difficult?"
- *Guided elimination, round 2 (4 min).* Students cross off a second word (down to 3). Pair discussion: "Which word was harder to eliminate and why?" Collect worksheets.
- *Reflection prompt.* "Was this process challenging? How might it connect to choices we make about which parts of ourselves to share in different situations?"

### Day 3: Visual Creation and Community Building (45-75 minutes)

**Learning objectives.** Students will be able to:

- Create an effective visual representation of personal identity
- Contribute meaningfully to a collaborative classroom environment
- Reflect on the identity exploration process

**Materials needed.** Art supplies (colored paper, markers, decorative materials); example word wall photos; 8.5" x 5.5" paper (consistent sizing).

**Activities**

- *Inspiration and instructions (5 min).* Show examples of previous word walls. "Your goal: create something that represents YOU and enhances OUR space." Size requirements and basic expectations.
- *Final word selection (5 min).* Redistribute worksheets. Students choose their ONE word from their final 2. Teacher conferences briefly with each student -- could just be students lining up for approval of their word before getting art supplies. For students struggling: ask about their interests, dreams, or what makes them proud. Provide gentle guidance, not directives (unless really necessary for a struggling student).
- *Creation and installation time (30-60 min).* Students design and create their word displays. Encourage creativity while maintaining classroom-appropriate content. Circulate and show genuine interest in their choices. Students add their words to the classroom wall.
- *Closing reflection (5 min).* "Look at our wall -- this represents the diversity and uniqueness of our learning community." "How might understanding each other's core identities help us work better together this year?"

### Day 4: Journal Reflection (10-15 minutes)

**Learning objectives.** Students will be able to:

- Reflect critically on their identity exploration process
- Connect personal identity work to classroom community building
- Articulate insights about decision-making and self-awareness

**Journal reflection prompts.** Answer both prompts in your journal (1-2 sentences each):

1. Why is your word important to you?
2. What's one word on our wall that makes you curious about that person?

(Wait to show prompt 3 until after most students have finished prompts 1 and 2.)

3. How would you explain your word to a parent, guardian, or caring adult at home?

**Activity.** Students spend 10-12 minutes writing. Optional sharing: 2-3 volunteers read one sentence from their reflection.

## Standards alignment

### Social-Emotional Learning (SEL) -- primary alignment

**California Transformative SEL Competencies (CDE, 2021).** Reference: [CDE T-SEL Competencies](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/se/tselcompetencies.asp).

**Self-Awareness** -- *"The abilities to understand one's own emotions, thoughts, and values and how they influence behavior across contexts. This includes capacities to recognize one's strengths and limitations with a well-grounded sense of confidence and purpose."*

- **Healthy and integrated identity development.** Students explore and articulate their personal identity through the selection of five words that describe them, fostering understanding of their unique characteristics, values, and experiences from their cultural, linguistic, and personal backgrounds.
- **Identifying and managing emotions.** Students reflect on emotions that arise during the challenging process of narrowing their identity words and practice expressing these feelings appropriately.
- **Demonstrating self-efficacy.** Students recognize their personal strengths, interests, and talents as they identify words that represent who they are.

**Self-Management** -- *"The abilities to harness one's emotions, thoughts, and behaviors effectively in different situations and to achieve goals and aspirations."*

- **Organizational skills and goal setting.** Students engage in the systematic process of prioritizing and eliminating identity words to reach the goal of selecting one representative word.
- **Self-discipline and motivation.** Students practice patience and persistence during the reflection process, managing frustration when required to eliminate meaningful identity aspects.
- **Building agency and hope.** Students develop confidence in their ability to represent themselves authentically while making difficult choices.

**Social Awareness** -- *"The abilities to understand the perspectives of and empathize with others, including those from diverse backgrounds, cultures, and contexts."*

- **Demonstrating empathy and compassion.** Students learn about diverse identities and backgrounds of classmates through shared word walls, developing understanding of different lived experiences.
- **Understanding and appreciating diversity.** Students see and celebrate the variety of words chosen by peers, recognizing how different cultural, linguistic, and personal backgrounds shape identity.
- **Recognizing strengths in others.** Students identify and appreciate the unique qualities and talents reflected in classmates' chosen words.

**Relationship Skills** -- *"The abilities to establish and maintain healthy and supportive relationships and to effectively navigate settings with diverse individuals and groups."*

- **Communicating effectively.** Students practice sharing personal information appropriately while respecting boundaries and listening respectfully to others' identity sharing.
- **Building and maintaining relationships.** Students connect with peers through discovering shared interests, experiences, or values reflected in chosen words.
- **Engaging in productive collaboration.** Students participate in collaborative discussions about identity and belonging, practicing respectful dialogue about personal characteristics.

**Responsible Decision-Making** -- *"The abilities to make caring and constructive choices about personal behavior and social interactions across diverse situations."*

- **Identifying problems and analyzing situations.** Students navigate the challenge of representing complex, multifaceted identity in limited words, considering multiple perspectives on what makes them unique.
- **Solving problems constructively.** Students develop strategies for making difficult choices about self-representation while honoring all aspects of their identity.
- **Reflecting on actions and decisions.** Students consider how their word choices and sharing contribute to building an inclusive, affirming classroom community.

### California Career Technical Education (CTE) standards

**Information and Communication Technologies (ICT) anchor standards.** Reference: [California CTE Model Curriculum Standards](https://www.cde.ca.gov/ci/ct/sf/documents/infocomtech.pdf) (CDE, 2013). As a CTE teacher, these anchor standards apply across all ICT pathways and are essential for college and career readiness.

**Anchor Standard 2.0: Communications**

- **2.5.** Communicate information and ideas effectively to multiple audiences using a variety of media and formats. *Application:* students share their identity words and reasoning through verbal presentation and visual display.
- **2.7.** Use technical writing and communication skills to work effectively with diverse groups of people. *Application:* students practice respectful communication when discussing personal identity and listening to peers' choices.
- **2.8.** Understand the principles of a customer-oriented service approach to users. *Application:* students develop empathy and understanding for diverse perspectives and backgrounds.

**Anchor Standard 3.0: Career Planning and Management**

- **3.1.** Identify personal interests, aptitudes, information, and skills necessary for informed career decision making. *Application:* students reflect on personal characteristics, interests, and strengths that inform future educational and career choices.
- **3.2.** Evaluate personal character traits such as trust, respect, and responsibility and understand the impact they can have on career success. *Application:* students identify and articulate personal values and character traits through their word selection process.

**Anchor Standard 7.0: Responsibility and Flexibility**

- **7.2.** Explain the importance of accountability and responsibility in fulfilling personal, community, and workplace roles. *Application:* students take ownership of their identity representation and participate responsibly in community-building activities.
- **7.3.** Understand the need to adapt to changing and varied roles and responsibilities. *Application:* students practice flexibility as they narrow their identity words and adapt to the collaborative classroom environment.

**Anchor Standard 8.0: Ethics and Legal Responsibilities**

- **8.4.** Explain the importance of personal integrity, confidentiality, and ethical behavior in the workplace. *Application:* students make ethical decisions about personal information sharing and respect others' privacy choices.
- **8.5.** Analyze organizational culture and practices within the workplace environment. *Application:* students contribute to creating an inclusive, respectful classroom culture that values diversity.

**Anchor Standard 9.0: Leadership and Teamwork**

- **9.3.** Understand the characteristics and benefits of teamwork, leadership, and citizenship in the school, community, and workplace setting. *Application:* students practice citizenship skills by contributing to a welcoming classroom community.
- **9.5.** Understand that the modern world is an international community and requires an expanded global view. *Application:* students appreciate diverse cultural backgrounds and perspectives represented in their classroom.
- **9.6.** Respect individual and cultural differences and recognize the importance of diversity in the workplace. *Application:* students celebrate the variety of identities, experiences, and backgrounds reflected in peer word choices.

**CTE integration value.** This lesson establishes foundational soft skills essential for success in Information and Communication Technologies careers, including communication, cultural competency, teamwork, and ethical decision-making. These competencies transfer directly to collaborative technology projects and diverse workplace environments.

### K-5 SEL standards met (for younger implementations)

**California Preschool Learning Foundations and Transitional Kindergarten.** Reference: California Preschool Learning Foundations (CDE, 2008); T-K Foundations (CDE, 2015). Social-emotional development: identity of self in relation to others (understanding personal characteristics and family background); social understanding (recognizing that people have different preferences, characteristics, and backgrounds); relationships (building positive relationships with peers and adults); self-regulation (managing emotions during potentially challenging reflection activities).

**Elementary adaptations (grades K-5).** Self-awareness development: recognizing personal interests, preferences, and characteristics; understanding that identity includes multiple aspects (family, interests, skills, etc.); beginning to articulate what makes them unique. Social skills building: listening respectfully to others' sharing; appreciating differences among classmates; contributing appropriately to group discussions.

### High school SEL standards met (grades 9-12)

**Advanced identity development.** Complex self-awareness: advanced identity integration (synthesizing multiple aspects of identity into coherent self-concept); values clarification (identifying core values and beliefs that guide decision-making); cultural identity (understanding how cultural background influences personal identity). Sophisticated social skills: empathy and perspective-taking (understanding how different life experiences shape identity); cultural competency (appreciating and respecting diverse backgrounds and experiences); leadership and advocacy (using understanding of identity to advocate for inclusive environments). Advanced decision-making: ethical reasoning (considering impact of identity expression on community building); goal alignment (connecting identity understanding to academic and career planning); social justice (understanding how identity intersects with equity and inclusion).

**College and career readiness connections.** Self-advocacy (articulating personal strengths and characteristics for college/job applications); professional identity (beginning to understand how personal identity translates to professional contexts); collaboration skills (working effectively with diverse teams and colleagues); communication (clearly expressing ideas and listening to diverse perspectives).
