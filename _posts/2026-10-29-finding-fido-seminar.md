Ring's Super Bowl ad shows a lost dog coming home. The camera feature behind it is on by default, and the founder keeps talking about ending crime. This is the simple version of my seminar on it, for grades 7 to 9. The ad is the whole seminar. It is built from [Finding Fido or Finding You?]({% post_url 2026-03-04-finding-fido-or-finding-you %}), and the AP CSP version is [You Deleted It. Is It Gone?]({% post_url 2026-10-22-you-deleted-it-is-it-gone %}).

I built it for a room where I hand-pick who is in the circle, because not every kid will be ready to carry a discussion. It also has an exit: Round 1 is a complete lesson, so if the class goes badly I can stop there and go straight to the exit.

## Learning goals

By the end of the seminar, students can:

1. Say who a default setting helps and who it costs, and why "on unless you turn it off" is a decision someone made.
2. Separate what a company says a feature is for from what its founder says he wants.
3. Use one piece of evidence from the reading, by paragraph number, and build on what a classmate said.
4. Explain what moved them, or held them in place.

## Materials

The format lives in the [Socratic Seminar Kit](/socratic-seminar-kit/?topic=Finding%20Fido). This seminar uses it with a lighter handout, a shorter list of norms, and a hand-picked circle. There is no hot seat.

- **Slides:** [Finding Fido](/finding-fido-slides/). The ad and both videos are embedded, each with a link to YouTube in case the embed does not play. Every stop slide has a red PAUSE HERE bar and a timer. The token rule is on the norms slide only. The reading is not on the slides. You hand it out and wait.
- **Reading, printable:** [The Dog Finder](/fido-reading-1/). The same text is below.
- **Handout:** [the kit's two-sided handout, in its shorter version](/socratic-seminar-handout/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=I%27d%20leave%20it%20on&b=I%27d%20turn%20it%20off&lite=1). One question, one piece of evidence, no best-argument question, and an observer organizer with two rows. The two sides on the entry ticket are "I'd leave it on" and "I'd turn it off," and the Start and End lines use the same two. It is graded out of 6.
- **Full handout (optional, for 9th graders who can take more):** [the kit's regular handout](/socratic-seminar-handout/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=I%27d%20leave%20it%20on&b=I%27d%20turn%20it%20off), graded out of 6.
- **Role cards (optional):** [four cards to a sheet](/socratic-seminar-cards/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=I%27d%20leave%20it%20on&ap=A%20lost%20dog%20comes%20home.%20%28Paragraph%201%29%0AThe%20neighbor%20decides%20whether%20to%20share%20the%20video.%20%28Paragraph%202%29%0ARing%20says%20it%20is%20only%20for%20pets.%20%28Paragraph%205%29&b=I%27d%20turn%20it%20off&bp=It%20is%20turned%20on%20automatically.%20Owners%20did%20not%20have%20to%20say%20yes.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AIt%20takes%20six%20steps%20to%20turn%20off.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AThe%20founder%20says%20Ring%27s%20real%20goal%20is%20to%20reduce%20crime.%20%28Paragraph%205%29), for students who will argue the side they did not pick. See "The opposite side" below.
- **Tokens:** three per student in the inner circle. Poker chips, paper slips, anything, plus a cup in the middle.
- **The ad:** [Ring's own upload on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OheUzrXsKrY).

**Teacher note:** the Wyze parody's fake reviews include an ICE reference. Preview it first. With 7th graders, pause before the reviews or use WeRateDogs instead.

## Norms

Post these and read them out loud.

1. One voice at a time.
2. Outside the circle, you are quiet. Your job is the observer organizer. (This is on the grading table.)
3. Inside the circle, you get 3 tokens. Each time you speak, you give one up. Use at least 2, and no more than 3. (The slide just says "Use your tokens." You explain the rule out loud.)

One more, for this seminar: sarcasm aimed at Ring is fair game. Sarcasm aimed at a classmate is a norm break, and it gets the kit's quiet consequence: you move to the outer circle and pick up the organizer.

The tokens are there so a few talkers cannot run the circle and nobody can hide. Inviting quieter voices in is my job, not the students'.

## Run of show

Minutes are from the start of class. The slide numbers are for [the deck](/finding-fido-slides/). A red PAUSE HERE bar marks the stop slides, and each has a timer you start with T.

| Slides | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 0–2 | Title, then the rubric. Read the rubric out loud. |
| 3–4 | 2–7 | **Opener.** Play the ad cold, then students go to one side of the room: "This is great" or "This is creepy," or the middle if they are unsure. Do not tell them you will ask again. |
| 5 | 7–9 | **Norms.** Read them out loud, add the sarcasm rule, hand tokens to the inner circle, and explain the token rule. |
| **6** | 9–14 | **Stop. Hand out [the reading](/fido-reading-1/) and the handout, then wait.** Students read it to themselves and fill in the entry ticket on the front. |
| **7** | 14–24 | **Stop. Round 1.** "Should Search Party be on by default?" Inside students finish the organizer right after. |
| 8–10 | 24–37, optional | **Round 2.** The Wyze video (slide 8), then WeRateDogs (slide 9), then slide 10: "If it can find a dog, could it find a person? Should it ever?" Skip a video with Next. Press S on any of these slides to skip to the exit. |
| **11** | 24–29, or 37–42 with Round 2 | **Stop. Exit.** A different question: "If it were your camera, which would you choose?" Students go to "I'd leave it on" or "I'd turn it off," then fill in the exit ticket on the back of the handout. |
| 12 | | Write-up links. |

That is about 29 minutes with Round 1 alone and about 42 with Round 2. These are estimates, not timed runs. Round 1 is a complete lesson. If the class goes badly, stop there and go straight to the exit.

## The fishbowl

You pick about 8 students for the inner circle. Say out loud that the circle rotates all year. Each gets 3 tokens. Everyone else is outside with the observer organizer on the back of the handout: an idea they agreed with and an idea they would push back on, each with who said it. Inside students fill in the organizer right after the round, from what they heard.

If you hold Round 2, pick a new circle with new tokens, or keep the same one.

## The opposite side

Optional, in Round 2. Look at the sides on the entry tickets. Ask one or two students to argue the side most of the room does not hold. Ask for volunteers first. Do not pick the most outspoken. Give each a [role card](/socratic-seminar-cards/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=I%27d%20leave%20it%20on&ap=A%20lost%20dog%20comes%20home.%20%28Paragraph%201%29%0AThe%20neighbor%20decides%20whether%20to%20share%20the%20video.%20%28Paragraph%202%29%0ARing%20says%20it%20is%20only%20for%20pets.%20%28Paragraph%205%29&b=I%27d%20turn%20it%20off&bp=It%20is%20turned%20on%20automatically.%20Owners%20did%20not%20have%20to%20say%20yes.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AIt%20takes%20six%20steps%20to%20turn%20off.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AThe%20founder%20says%20Ring%27s%20real%20goal%20is%20to%20reduce%20crime.%20%28Paragraph%205%29), say out loud who is playing a role, and afterward ask what it was like to argue a side they did not pick.

## The rounds

Say the question, then stay quiet. Reach for a back-pocket question only if the circle stalls. The slides show just the question.

**Round 1: Should Search Party be on by default?**

- Back-pocket: Who is on that video besides the owner? The delivery driver, a neighbor, a guest. Do they get a say?
- Back-pocket: Who benefits when the setting starts "on"? Who is harmed?

**Round 2: If it can find a dog, could it find a person? Should it ever?**

Play the [Wyze parody](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROFblZ_-9q4) (about 70 seconds), then [WeRateDogs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ITnLiqKbeI) (about 3 minutes). Both are embedded in the slides. Neither video is a neutral source. One is a competitor's ad and one is a creator arguing a position, and that is worth saying out loud.

- Back-pocket: Wyze makes cameras too. Does that change how much you trust its parody?
- Back-pocket: Ring says pets only. The founder talks about ending crime. Which do you weigh more?

## Grading

The handout has its own grading table on the front. In the shorter version it is out of 6, the same as the kit's regular handout:

| | Points |
|---|---|
| Entry ticket: one question, and a side with a reason | 1 |
| Entry ticket: one piece of evidence, with a paragraph number | 1 |
| Your seat: inside, you spent 2 or 3 tokens; outside, you stayed quiet | 2 |
| Observer organizer: two rows, each naming who said it | 1 |
| Exit ticket: Start and End, and a reason that names what was said or asked in the room | 1 |

I grade evidence and honesty, not the side picked. A student who changes sides because of something said in the room did the thing the seminar is for. The rubric is also on the second slide of the deck.
