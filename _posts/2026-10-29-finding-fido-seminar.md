Ring's Super Bowl ad shows a lost dog coming home. The camera feature behind it is on by default, and the founder keeps talking about ending crime. This is the simple version of my seminar on it, for grades 7 to 9. The ad is the whole seminar. It is built from [Finding Fido or Finding You?]({% post_url 2026-03-04-finding-fido-or-finding-you %}), and the AP CSP version is [You Deleted It. Is It Gone?]({% post_url 2026-10-22-you-deleted-it-is-it-gone %}).

I built it for a room where I hand-pick who is in the circle, because not every kid will be ready to carry a discussion. It also has an exit: Round 1 is a complete lesson, so if the class goes badly I can stop there and go straight to the exit.

## Learning goals

By the end of the seminar, students can:

1. Say who a default setting helps and who it costs, and why "on unless you turn it off" is a decision someone made.
2. Separate what a company says a feature is for from what its founder says he wants.
3. Use one piece of evidence from the reading, by paragraph number, and build on what a classmate said.
4. Explain what moved them, or held them in place.

## Materials

The format lives in the [Socratic Seminar Kit](/socratic-seminar-kit/?topic=Finding%20Fido). This seminar uses it with a lighter handout, a shorter list of norms, and a hand-picked circle. There is no hot seat.

- **Slides:** [Finding Fido](/finding-fido-slides/). The ad and both videos are embedded, each with a link to YouTube in case the embed does not play. Every stop slide has a red PAUSE HERE bar, and most have a timer. Tokens are optional, and the norms slide does not mention them. The reading is not on the slides. You hand it out and wait.
- **Reading, printable:** [The Dog Finder](/fido-reading-1/). The same text is below.
- **Handout:** [the kit's two-sided handout, in its shorter version](/socratic-seminar-handout/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=On%20by%20default&b=Off%20by%20default&c=Ask%20each%20owner&entry=Does%20your%20home%20have%20a%20doorbell%20camera%3F%20About%20how%20many%20do%20you%20walk%20past%20to%20get%20to%20school%3F&lite=1). One question, one piece of evidence, no best-argument question, and an observer organizer with four rows to choose from, of which students fill in any two. The entry ticket also asks: "Does your home have a doorbell camera? About how many do you walk past to get to school?" (the handout link carries it). The side on the entry ticket, and the Start and End lines, use the same three choices as the corner slide: "On by default," "Off by default," and "Ask each owner." It is graded out of 6.
- **Full handout (optional, for 9th graders who can take more):** [the kit's regular handout](/socratic-seminar-handout/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=On%20by%20default&b=Off%20by%20default&c=Ask%20each%20owner&entry=Does%20your%20home%20have%20a%20doorbell%20camera%3F%20About%20how%20many%20do%20you%20walk%20past%20to%20get%20to%20school%3F), graded out of 6.
- **Role cards (optional):** [four cards to a sheet](/socratic-seminar-cards/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=On%20by%20default&ap=A%20lost%20dog%20comes%20home.%20%28Paragraph%201%29%0AThe%20neighbor%20decides%20whether%20to%20share%20the%20video.%20%28Paragraph%202%29%0ARing%20says%20it%20is%20only%20for%20pets.%20%28Paragraph%205%29&b=Off%20by%20default&bp=It%20is%20turned%20on%20automatically.%20Owners%20did%20not%20have%20to%20say%20yes.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AIt%20takes%20six%20steps%20to%20turn%20off.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AThe%20founder%20says%20Ring%27s%20real%20goal%20is%20to%20reduce%20crime.%20%28Paragraph%205%29), for students who will argue the side they did not pick. See "The opposite side" below.
- **Tokens (optional):** three per student in the inner circle. Poker chips, paper slips, anything, plus a cup in the middle.
- **The ad:** [Ring's own upload on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OheUzrXsKrY).

**Teacher note:** the Wyze parody's fake reviews include an ICE reference. Preview it first. With 7th graders, pause before the reviews, or use WeRateDogs part 1 instead.

**Teacher note:** the WeRateDogs video names ICE at about 0:44 (neighbors as informants for ICE and other agencies), and from 1:34 it gives examples of ICE and Texas police doing warrantless searches, including a search for a woman over an abortion. Preview it. With 7th graders, stop before 1:34 (show parts 1 and 2), or before 0:44 (part 1 only) if you want ICE out of it entirely. The slides split WeRateDogs into four clips, so you can show some and skip the rest. Or use the Wyze parody alone, paused before the fake reviews.

## Norms

Post these and read them out loud.

1. One voice at a time.
2. Outside the circle, you are quiet. Your job is to listen and take notes on the observer organizer. (This is on the grading table.)
3. Optional: Inside the circle, you get 3 tokens. Each time you speak, you give one up. Use at least 2, and no more than 3. (Not on the slide. You explain the rule out loud. Without tokens, say it as a count: speak 2 or 3 times.)

One more, for this seminar: sarcasm aimed at Ring is fair game. Sarcasm aimed at a classmate is a norm break, and it gets the kit's quiet consequence: you move to the outer circle and pick up the organizer.

The tokens (if you use them) are there so a few talkers cannot run the circle and nobody can hide. Inviting quieter voices in is my job, not the students'.

## Run of show

Minutes are from the start of class. The slide numbers are for [the deck](/finding-fido-slides/). A red PAUSE HERE bar marks the stop slides, and each timed one has a timer. The first press of Next or the right arrow on a timed slide starts it (T also works).

| Slides | Min | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–1 | Title. |
| **2** | 1–4 | <strong>Stop. Three questions, before the ad.</strong> Dog or cat person, ever lost a pet, how many doorbell cameras do you walk past to get to school. No timer. Each press of Next adds one question. Take a few answers each. |
| 3 | 4–5 | **Opener.** Play the ad cold, with no setup about Ring or the camera. |
| 4 | 5–7 | Show of hands, nobody moves: "This is great" or "This is creepy." Count each side. Do not tell them you will ask again. |
| 5–6 | 7–10 | **Rubric, then norms.** Read the rubric out loud. Read the norms and add the sarcasm rule. Tokens are optional; if you use them, hand them out and explain the rule. |
| **7** | 10–20 | **Stop. Hand out [the reading](/fido-reading-1/) and the handout, then wait.** Students read it to themselves and fill in the entry ticket on the front, including the new doorbell camera line. Ten minutes, up from five. |
| 8 | 20–21 | <strong>"Same camera. Same AI."</strong> Three pictures of what a doorbell camera sees: a dog, a delivery driver, and a kid walking to school (AI-generated, fictional people). No other text. |
| 9 | 21–24 | **Take a position.** "You're Ring's engineers. Which would you build?" Three corners: on by default (what Ring did), off by default, or ask each owner. Owners can change the setting either way. Students go to the corner they circled on the entry ticket. Then you choose the inner circle from all three corners, and they take their seats. Everyone else sits down with the organizer. |
| **10** | 24–34 | **Stop. Round 1.** "Should Search Party be on by default?" The slide adds "Remember your camera count." Inside students finish the organizer right after. |
| 11–16, optional | 34–47 | **Round 2.** The Wyze video (slide 11), then WeRateDogs in four clips (slides 12 to 15), then slide 16: "If it can find a dog, could it find a person? Should it ever?" Skip a clip with Next. Press S on any of these slides to skip to the exit. |
| **17** | 34–39, or 47–52 with Round 2 | **Stop. Exit.** The same question and corners as slide 9. Students go to a corner again, then fill in the exit ticket on the back of the handout. Point at the corner most students chose and ask: "So why didn't Ring do that?" Compare with where they stood before Round 1. |
| 18 | | Write-up links. |

That is about 39 minutes with Round 1 alone and about 52 with Round 2. These are estimates, not timed runs. Round 1 can stand alone, but only if the room split. See the facilitation note below.

## The fishbowl

You pick about 8 students for the inner circle. Say out loud that the circle rotates all year. If you use tokens, each gets 3. Everyone else is outside with the observer organizer on the back of the handout: an idea they agreed with, an idea they would push back on, the strongest point made, and a question nobody has asked yet. They fill in any two, each with who said it. Inside students fill in the organizer right after the round, from what they heard.

If you hold Round 2, pick a new circle (with new tokens, if you use them), or keep the same one.

## The opposite side

Optional, in Round 2. Look at where the room stood on slide 9. Ask one or two students to argue the side most of the room does not hold (the cards cover "on" and "off"; with three corners, pick whichever of those two the room left emptiest). Ask for volunteers first. Do not pick the most outspoken. Give each a [role card](/socratic-seminar-cards/?topic=Finding%20Fido&a=On%20by%20default&ap=A%20lost%20dog%20comes%20home.%20%28Paragraph%201%29%0AThe%20neighbor%20decides%20whether%20to%20share%20the%20video.%20%28Paragraph%202%29%0ARing%20says%20it%20is%20only%20for%20pets.%20%28Paragraph%205%29&b=Off%20by%20default&bp=It%20is%20turned%20on%20automatically.%20Owners%20did%20not%20have%20to%20say%20yes.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AIt%20takes%20six%20steps%20to%20turn%20off.%20%28Paragraph%203%29%0AThe%20founder%20says%20Ring%27s%20real%20goal%20is%20to%20reduce%20crime.%20%28Paragraph%205%29), say out loud who is playing a role, and afterward ask what it was like to argue a side they did not pick.

## The rounds

Say the question, then stay quiet. Reach for a back-pocket question only if the circle stalls. The slides show just the question.

**Round 1: Should Search Party be on by default?**

- Back-pocket: Who is on that video besides the owner? The delivery driver, a neighbor, a guest. Do they get a say?
- Back-pocket: Who benefits when the setting starts "on"? Who is harmed?
- Back-pocket: "Your neighbor's camera films you every day walking home. Should Ring's AI be able to search that video without asking you?"
- Back-pocket: "Why only dogs? Why not cats? If it can learn cats, what else could it learn to find?"

<strong>If the circle all agrees, don't wait.</strong> Keep Round 1 short, play the video, then ask the same question again. The video is what creates the disagreement. With my 9th graders, almost the whole room thought Search Party was a great idea, and the whole room flipped to "creepy" after the second video.

**Round 2: If it can find a dog, could it find a person? Should it ever?**

Play the [Wyze parody](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ROFblZ_-9q4) (about 70 seconds), then [WeRateDogs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-ITnLiqKbeI) (about 3 minutes; the slides split it into four clips, so you can show some and not all). Both are embedded in the slides. Neither video is a neutral source. One is a competitor's ad and one is a creator arguing a position, and that is worth saying out loud.

- Back-pocket: Wyze makes cameras too. Does that change how much you trust its parody?
- Back-pocket: Ring says pets only. The founder talks about ending crime. Which do you weigh more?

## Grading

The handout has its own grading table on the front. In the shorter version it is out of 6, the same as the kit's regular handout:

| | Points |
|---|---|
| Entry ticket: one question, and a side with a reason | 1 |
| Entry ticket: one piece of evidence, with a paragraph number | 1 |
| Your seat: inside, you spoke 2 or 3 times; outside, you stayed quiet | 2 |
| Observer organizer: two rows, each naming who said it | 1 |
| Exit ticket: Start and End, and a reason that names what was said or asked in the room | 1 |

I grade evidence and honesty, not the side picked. A student who changes sides because of something said in the room did the thing the seminar is for. The rubric is also on the second slide of the deck.
