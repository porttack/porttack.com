My daughter's teacher at her Waldorf school pointed me at [Cyber Civics](https://www.cybercivics.com/). It's the first digital citizenship curriculum I've seen that I'd actually want to teach.

<!--more-->

Most of what's out there, Common Sense Media included, is organized around risk. Passwords, predators, privacy settings. Useful material, but it treats kids as potential victims and the internet as weather that happens to them.

Cyber Civics starts somewhere else. It does the real-world community work before it goes online, and it puts creating ahead of consuming. The bet is that a kid who has practiced being a participant offline will be one online.

I bought the curriculum for my son's middle school and taught several lessons before the pandemic ended that. The classes went better than I expected. Middle schoolers have plenty to say about this stuff when you ask them instead of warning them.

It's also [aligned to some Common Core ELA standards](https://www.cybercivics.com/educational-standards-alignment), which matters when you're trying to find room for something in a schedule that's already full.

If you want the background rather than the curriculum, Diana Graber's [Raising Humans in a Digital World](https://www.cyberwise.org/book) is the book. She wrote Cyber Civics, and she was teaching it to middle schoolers while she developed it, which shows.
